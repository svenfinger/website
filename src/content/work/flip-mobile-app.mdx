During my first month at Flip, I audited the app through everyday use, documenting friction in navigation, search, and common interactions. I translated those fresh impressions into concrete proposals and presented them to the Product and R&D team.

The focus was on practical improvements we could make to the existing product, from helping people find content to making better use of limited screen space.

  <WorkText title="Making it easier to get started">

Getting started involved finding the right company, signing in, and setting up the app. I refined that journey with clearer guidance and a welcome experience that worked for both Flip and customer-branded apps.

I also reworked how we introduced account setup and preferences. Contextual reminders let people address notifications, recovery details, and appearance after entering the app, reducing the competing prompts at first launch.

</WorkText>

The supporting states mattered too: requesting microphone access, explaining how to restore it, and helping people retry a failed upload.

  <WorkText title="Celebrating milestones">

As part of a hackathon, I explored achievements as a way to encourage employees to return to the app each week. Weekly streaks turned regular visits into visible progress, with badges marking milestones from two weeks to a full year.

The badges evolved in color and shape as streaks grew, supported by progress indicators and celebratory unlock moments. I also explored achievements for participation, such as reacting to posts and commenting.

</WorkText>

For customer-branded apps, I explored public areas that people could access before signing in. The work addressed the app-review constraints we faced while considering how to introduce a company and provide a clear route into the employee experience.

In a separate hackathon, I explored weekly streaks and achievements as a way to encourage return visits. The concept used progress and milestone feedback to recognize regular use.
